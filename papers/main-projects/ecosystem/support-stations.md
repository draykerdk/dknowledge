# Support stations, centers, and embassies

Where Drayker meets physical ground: how distributed infrastructure expands across ordinary cities today versus long-term bilateral embassies and autonomous maritime research zones. This paper synthesizes the current public distinction behind [Stations](https://stations.drayker.org). It describes physical-layer planning principles, not active facilities operated today.

## Canonical separation: Stations vs embassies vs autonomous zones

Early notes occasionally blended ordinary workspace hubs with autonomous civic treaties. Current architecture enforces clear legal and spatial boundaries across three tiers:

1. **Support stations, centers, and buildings (no special sovereign territory needed):** ordinary properties, laboratories, workshops, co-working spaces, clinics, or dedicated research buildings operating legally inside existing cities and countries anywhere in the world. They may be formed by local member teams, partner cooperatives, or collective pooled acquisition and leasing — including voluntary pooled percentages from members' mutual-aid capacity.
2. **Bilateral embassies in ceded territory (long-term bilateral accords):** formal territory agreements negotiated reciprocally with willing nation-states and backed by consent of the surrounding local population — functioning similarly to chartered autonomy zones that include **member housing**, deep R&D complexes, health clinics, and academy campuses. These are constitutionally distinct from the founding *Drayker Embassy* coordination nucleus.
3. **Pure autonomous ocean zones (long-horizon maritime R&D):** purpose-built sea infrastructure in international waters dedicated to clean experimental hardware testing, energy research, and neutral global redundancy when land jurisdiction proves too restrictive.

## Architectural typology for ground facilities

Within cities and local stations, facilities adapt to concrete territorial needs rather than rigid franchising:

- **General mixed-use buildings:** accessible lower floors housing open autonomous community kitchens or services, paired with upper stories dedicated to R&D hardware workshops, `Dk Academy` classrooms, `Autonomous Health` clinics, and urban vertical agriculture.
- **Single-purpose dedicated centers:** specialized sites focused on satellite/full-node data custody, biomedical or materials research, clinical care, or affordable student/fellow residences.
- **Nomadic contributor hospitality:** verified itinerant contributors traveling between regions receive workspace, connectivity, and temporary shelter backed by contextual network trust rather than corporate travel budgets.

## Allocation between Main Fund and Support Fund

Physical rollout follows strict separation between long-horizon core infrastructure and immediate member well-being:

- The **Main Fund** anchors structural ecosystem milestones — maintaining at most one flagship territorial embassy initiative in active build phase alongside smaller planning reserves preparing subsequent hubs.
- The **Support Fund** deploys resources to city-level stations, member clinics, academy programs, and local survival/quality-of-life hubs wherever concrete execution effort, financial transparency, and replicable local value are demonstrated.
