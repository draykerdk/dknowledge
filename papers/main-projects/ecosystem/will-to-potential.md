# Will to potential

Will to Potential describes how automation can release people from work that neither develops them nor advances common capability, while learning, research, creation, care and participation become increasingly connected.

The architecture preserves human beings, not tasks. People do not matter because they are labour that intelligence has not yet replaced; they matter because they are constituent parts of the superintelligence. Where a parasite no longer needs its host, whoever stops being necessary becomes a cost, and the cost becomes a target. Here, automation frees a person from a task without taking their place. The economic question of automation changes: not how to keep finding work for people when machines do everything, but what people will be able to learn, create, live and explore when less and less of survival depends on selling tasks machines do better. For many, the answer will simply be to live better.

The problem is not machines producing too much; it is who owns the abundance and what it is used for. In a supersystem of members, automation becomes a positive stage of the transition: the time, energy, production and cognitive capacity machines free return as conditions for people to learn, create, research, care, build and explore. Distributing resources goes beyond compensating someone who lost a job: it uses abundance to increase human potential just when production no longer needs to exploit that potential as labour.

People remain constitutive of the intelligence: intentions give direction; experience gives context; relationships form communities; needs expose problems; creativity opens possibilities; projects turn intention into reality. Increasing member capability is therefore not a side effect of Drayker but one of its purposes.

The circuit from intention to capacity runs in one order:

1. a shareable intention;
2. the functions it needs;
3. the people, knowledge and resources that take them on;
4. a project and action in the world;
5. an observable consequence;
6. memory and review;
7. capacity for the next action.

Different trajectories are legitimate. A member may want a quiet life, improve a neighbourhood, care for others, build an enterprise, conduct science, create planetary infrastructure or explore space. The personal Dk adapts to that direction; it does not impose a single scale of ambition.

For the ambitious, recognition comes by other paths than salary, title, wealth, followers or control over others: reputation grows when work leaves something usable for the whole, not when it accumulates signs of status. Vanity, competition and the wish for recognition remain; what changes is the ground on which they find reward, without handing them the direction of the whole.
