# Distributed Support

This is the current public formulation of Distributed Support in relation to the whole Drayker system.

## Systemic role

Support connects members, projects, communities and infrastructure to the capacity that lets them participate, learn, build and recover. Its human plane includes formation, tools, time, places, project resources, assistance under need and projects of life. Its infrastructure plane includes compute, storage, bandwidth, maintenance, redundancy and Stations.

A project of life relates a member's intentions, dreams, studies, experience, relationships and projects through the personal Dk, PAP and Dknowledge. It can be simple and local or highly ambitious; the system adapts to the person's direction rather than forcing one measure of ambition.

## Current economic interpretation

The internal economy is a capacity economy, not a market among members. A future allocation can relate need, intention, contextual reputation, knowledge, trust, project quality, urgency, risk, expected impact and resource availability. Dk performs synthesis and decides and executes allocations autonomously inside the constitution; Dknowledge preserves reasons and consequences; members can make a well-justified veto, which obliges review.

Synthesis alone does not authorize an action. Systemic safeguards — constitutional constraints, security, permissions, integrity, reserves and risk — can limit, postpone, compartmentalize or block what Dk proposes, so the executable decision is always the one inside a permitted and safe space. Effects, refusals and consequences return through members' personal Dks as new evidence, without a universal individual veto. Every refusal carries its grounds, even when its author stays anonymous. A well-founded pattern of refusals can justify review, and so can a single refusal whose grounds bring information beyond the scope considered before the decision, once an advanced triage confirms what that information is and what it means ([veto chain](https://uid.drayker.org)). A decision can then be maintained, adapted, excepted, suspended or reversed.

Access to the common floor of possibility belongs to every active member. While the network cannot sustain it for everyone, mainly when resources are scarce, access depends on an endorsement that works more like a queue weighted by context, such as each person's need and urgency, than like an approval in itself; at the start, contextual reputation helps protect the network against abuse and prioritize those who have shown integration, cooperation and responsibility. As capacity grows, what depended on the queue becomes part of the floor, and reputation comes to weigh only on what remains scarce. Reputation does not determine membership, dignity or fundamental rights.
