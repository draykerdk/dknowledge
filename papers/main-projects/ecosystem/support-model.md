# Distributed Support

This is the current public formulation of Distributed Support in relation to the whole Drayker system.

## Systemic role

Distributed Support is a program and application for supporting members at multiple levels, locally and globally. It seeks to relate needs to material, social, educational, computational, community and other capacities, including distributed infrastructure as one of its fronts. Support connects members, projects, communities and infrastructure to the capacity that lets them participate, learn, build and recover. Its human plane includes formation, tools, time, places, project resources, assistance under need and projects of life. Its infrastructure plane includes compute, storage, bandwidth, maintenance, redundancy and Stations.

A project of life relates a member's intentions, dreams, studies, experience, relationships and projects through the personal Dk, PAP and Dknowledge. It can be simple and local or highly ambitious; the system adapts to the person's direction rather than forcing one measure of ambition.

## Current economic interpretation

The internal economy is a capacity economy, not a market among members: an internal architecture of allocation in which need, intention, reputation, knowledge, resources, risk, opportunity and consequence are related so as to increase the capacities of the members and of the whole. A future allocation can relate need, intention, contextual reputation, knowledge, trust, project quality, urgency, risk, expected impact and resource availability. Dk performs synthesis and decides and executes allocations autonomously inside the constitution; Dknowledge preserves reasons and consequences; members can make a well-justified veto, which obliges review.

Synthesis alone does not authorize an action. Systemic safeguards — constitutional, security, permission, integrity, reserve and risk limits that condition the Dk's decisions and actions — keep a contextual or reputational synthesis from turning automatically into execution when the general state of the system makes the action unsafe, illegitimate or premature. They can limit, postpone, compartmentalize or block what Dk proposes, so the executable decision is always the one inside a permitted and safe space. Effects, refusals and consequences return through members' personal Dks as new evidence, without a universal individual veto. Every refusal carries its grounds, even when its author stays anonymous, and is weighed by what grounds it, not counted as a vote. A well-founded pattern of refusals can justify review, and so can a single refusal whose grounds bring information beyond the scope considered before the decision, once an advanced triage confirms what that information is and what it means ([veto chain](https://uid.drayker.org)). A decision can then be maintained, adapted, excepted, suspended or reversed.

The common floor of possibility is the basics for living, studying and communicating (food, shelter, health, education, information, communication and basic computing capacity), which the network guarantees to every active member, to the extent the infrastructure can sustain it. While the network cannot sustain it for everyone, above all when resources are scarce, access depends on an endorsement, which works more like a queue weighted by context, such as each person's need and urgency, than like an approval as such; at the beginning of the network, contextual reputation helps protect the organism against abuse and prioritize those who have shown integration, cooperation and responsibility. It grows with abundance: what once depended on a queue becomes part of the floor, and reputation weighs only on what remains scarce. Reputation does not determine membership, dignity or fundamental rights.
