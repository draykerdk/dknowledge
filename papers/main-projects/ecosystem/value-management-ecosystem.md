# Economy of capacity

The economy of capacity connects project funds, categories, incentives, reputation and Dk-assisted contextual allocation into one learning system.

None of the loop described below runs today. In the DAF's current phase, resource requests are filed and decided in its assembly, and the federation holds no treasury. The loop describes Dk Global from version 1.0, whose economic calculation improves with every cycle because the intelligence that computes it keeps improving itself.

## The economic corpus

The economy feeds on a corpus no market or planner ever had. Personal Dks bring needs, intentions, skills and the local knowledge of each life; local Dks bring the state of each project and community and what each could do with more resources; projects bring plans, deadlines, dependencies and results; the network brings the state of resources — energy, computing, equipment, stocks, logistics; devices bring signals from the world in real time; Dk Global's forecasts bring what tends to happen; and the priorities deliberated by members say what comes first. Knowledge dispersed across millions of lives does not have to be compressed into a price to circulate.

## Allocation as a learning loop

Allocation follows an ordered loop:

1. **Factors.** Need, intention, ability, contextual reputation, knowledge, project quality, resources, opportunity, urgency, risk, expected impact and expected consequence become evidence. Dknowledge keeps the operational ontology and provenance.
2. **Local and global context.** The factors are placed in their local context and in the global one.
3. **Contextual synthesis.** Dk relates the factors within that context.
4. **Priority.** Priorities deliberated by members in advance say what comes first; the synthesis applies them to the case. Everyday decisions do not wait for members to weigh each request.
5. **Allocation and support.** Dk decides the allocation and executes it autonomously inside the constitution. Members can make a well-justified veto, which obliges Dk to review the decision. What only a person can say — the value they give to things and what they accept to lose — enters through justified proposals and vetoes. A proposal that brings it can change the decision before the veto is needed.
6. **Consequence and new learning.** Results return to Dknowledge and change later decisions.

Synthesis alone does not authorize an action. Every proposed allocation passes through **systemic safeguards** — constitutional constraints, security, permissions, integrity, reserves and risk — that can limit, postpone, compartmentalize or block what Dk proposes. The result is an executable decision inside a permitted and safe space, not an automatic conversion of context into action.

Representation does not end when a decision is taken. Effects, refusals and consequences return through the members' personal Dks as new evidence for local and global synthesis. A single refusal is never a universal individual veto, but it is never mere noise either: every refusal carries its grounds, even when its author stays anonymous. A well-founded pattern of refusals or unexpected consequences can justify review, and so can a single refusal whose grounds bring information beyond the scope considered before the decision, once an advanced triage confirms what that information is and what it means ([veto chain](https://uid.drayker.org)). A decision can then be maintained, adapted, given exceptions, suspended or reversed in light of the new state of the system.

Incentives are not fixed. A community that finds a mechanism encourages destructive competition can revise it; a project that shows cross-cooperation works better can make that behaviour weigh more. The system learns not only who contributed but which ecologies of incentive make members and communities more capable.

Auditability does not mean total exposure. Personal context, private projects, sensitive research, critical infrastructure and open knowledge can have different permissions; authorized Dk processes may use information that is not generally disclosed, while the existence and legitimacy of the decision path remain reviewable. A relevant allocation leaves an examinable path between available resources, requests considered, criteria applied and result, including who authorised execution and what happened after. For a member, the entry point is simple: learn the relevant reason, correct a piece of information and follow what happens to the challenge.

Security conditions normal decisions, not only attacks: what may be accessed, what may be executed, how much capacity may be committed, when a decision must escalate to human, local or global review. Security must not become the fiction that immunizes the system against correction: legitimate criticism is not infiltration, internal error is not sabotage, and extraordinary measures leave enough memory for later review.

## Reputation

Contextual reputation is a base layer of the architecture and is present in almost everything: data, relations, functions and members. It is the evolving form of the system's metadata. It grows as it proves valid against other information and in the real world, and falls when the world contradicts it. It can express trust, integration, validation in reality, contribution and use, and each type has its own rules of evaluation, deterministic or intelligent. It links almost everything in the architecture, governance, the distribution of resources and external relations.

For members, reputation is multidimensional memory, not one moral score. It can include general integration, local cooperation, reliability, learning, knowledge contribution, execution of functions, responsible use of resources, technical capacity by domain, care and social support, and project history, along with useful dissent and recovery. Each decision consults the dimension that concerns it, and no single metric comes to stand for the person. Not every contribution has to be large: taking part in learning, helping people nearby, caring well for a small responsibility or simply remaining a constructive member long enough also produces history.

A new member has little history, not proof of little ability. Any cumulative reputation risks closing the system: whoever has history receives more capacity and produces more history. New members therefore need paths to build trust without competing at once with those who have taken part for years: education, local cooperation, small functions, experimental projects, capacity grants, limited resources and low-exposure opportunities. Not all capacity follows only what has already proved to work; part of it stays reserved to discover new people, ideas and paths.

Trying and failing, hiding information, abandoning a responsibility without saying so, fraud, exploiting members and deliberately sabotaging the system are different relations and must not collapse into one penalty. If an attempt was legitimate, transparent and left reusable learning, its failure increases the system's capacity. A serious fault reduces trust and may require limits; repair, time and new consequences widen participation again. Memory serves to learn, not to freeze people at the worst moment of their histories.

Well-grounded criticism never becomes a reputation cost. Whoever disagrees with the direction of the whole does not need to leave to show they were right: they can test their proposal in parallel, as a project, and what is validated becomes part of the system.

A general level may summarize integration; it cannot replace the underlying trajectory. The multidimensional history remains the real source, and the level is a useful compression that the system can always look behind. A longer history of contribution tends to give higher priority in queues for scarce resources, especially for projects, and the maturity of each person's and each project's Dknowledge makes the request that reaches the queue more precise. A high level increases trust in a relation and stays subject to the context of each request. A low level marks a relation with little history, and history is built.

Caring for an elderly person, organising a community space, teaching a child or repairing something everyone uses may produce no global project, yet they sustain the fabric in which global projects become possible. Local Dks help keep that knowledge, general reputation receives part of that history with the local context that gives it meaning, and Dk Global sees the value of each scale.

Reputation can influence scarce capacity and responsibility. It cannot define human worth, membership or the right to exist.

One kind of reputation works differently: the reward. Whoever contributes capacity, such as computing lent to the network, and whoever finances the network receive a reputation that can be passed on and that gives faster or priority access to resources. It is the only transferable reputation. It carries no governance weight and never weighs in members' constitutional choices, which remain weighted by the reputation that is not transferred.

Dk Global inherits the reactivity of its members and does not erase it; what is learned from it stays recorded. The use reputation of that material comes only from what it teaches as perspective, without being taken as the real. The weighting weighs reasons, not volume: an impulse that arrives without reasons enters as a signal, not as a direction.

### Rights are not prizes

The economy of capacity begins with a constitutional separation. Some things follow from being a member: base representation by Dk Personal, continuity of identity, and the possibility to learn, take part, form relations, contribute and contest. Others depend on trust, scarcity and responsibility. As material capacity grows, a growing base of essential needs crosses that line and becomes a right sustained by the whole, as the common floor. Priority over scarce capacity results from the full context: who needs it and how urgently, what project asks, what impact it promises, what history and trust it has, and what competence it has for the requested use.

### Collusion and capture

Any measure that grants access invites people to optimize the measure instead of the work, and groups can validate one another. The defence follows from how contextual reputation grows: by proving valid against other information and in the real world. Validation therefore counts by its independence. Its weight falls when it comes from people who only validate one another, and rises when it comes from distant, independent contexts and from tests in the physical world. Dk looks for echo in the graph of validations as it does in knowledge.

The same defences hold against poisoning, when coordinated sources fabricate patterns for Dk Global to learn from. Each UID corresponds to one living person, which keeps crowds of fake accounts out of the network ([UID](https://uid.drayker.org)). A pattern learned from personal Dks gains weight only as it is validated independently in more instances ([Dk Personal](https://personal.drayker.org)). The triage of the veto chain detects coordinated vetoes and fabricated grounds, the echo in the graph of validations lowers the weight of groups that only validate one another, and the detection of anomalous behaviour is a security requirement of [Dk Network](https://dknetwork.drayker.org). What lies beyond the independence of contexts remains an open question ([What this does not solve](../dk/constitutional-alignment.md#what-this-does-not-solve)).

Proven collusion is misconduct, not failure: it sits with fraud, not with honest error, and the [DAF constitution](https://github.com/draykerdk/daf/blob/master/dafp/daf-000-federation-constitution.md) already sanctions it (DAF-000 §7).

The weight of a validation follows the evidence that continues to sustain it, and the history is never deleted. There is no decay through inactivity, and a justified pause does not remove a level, as with federative points (DAF-000 §3.3 and §3.4).

Whoever vouches for a member, a result or a UID risks part of their own reputation only in proven fraud or collusion, never in honest error, so that vouching does not close the door to new members.

### External relations

Ethics continues when each decision is traced to those truly responsible. Just as within Drayker the weight of a reputation carries how much responsibility someone can take on, in external relations the real responsibility for actions defines the reputation of those we interact with, and consequences go directly to those responsible. How the responsibility of states, corporations and other supersystems is traced, recorded and weighed in diplomatic, economic and security interfaces is still to be specified.

## Scarcity and abundance

Contextual allocation helps build more capacity. As a resource becomes abundant, it should leave the scarcity queue and join a rising common floor. Reputation then moves toward what remains rare, risky or responsibility-heavy. This movement keeps merit from becoming an endless competition for basic needs. Abundance brings back an old risk, comfort dissolving cohesion, but the rising floor does not erase the edge where responsibility and risk remain, and there the cohesive core keeps forming.
