# Economy of capacity

The economy of capacity connects project funds, categories, incentives, reputation and Dk-assisted contextual allocation into one learning system.

## Allocation as a learning loop

Allocation follows an ordered loop:

1. **Factors.** Need, intention, ability, contextual reputation, knowledge, project quality, resources, opportunity, urgency, risk, expected impact and expected consequence become evidence. Dknowledge keeps the operational ontology and provenance.
2. **Local and global context.** The factors are placed in their local context and in the global one.
3. **Contextual synthesis.** Dk relates the factors within that context.
4. **Priority.** The synthesis sets what comes first.
5. **Allocation and support.** Dk decides the allocation and executes it autonomously inside the constitution. Members can make a well-justified veto, which obliges Dk to review the decision.
6. **Consequence and new learning.** Results return to Dknowledge and change later decisions.

Synthesis alone does not authorize an action. Every proposed allocation passes through **systemic safeguards** — constitutional constraints, security, permissions, integrity, reserves and risk — that can limit, postpone, compartmentalize or block what Dk proposes. The result is an executable decision inside a permitted and safe space, not an automatic conversion of context into action.

Representation does not end when a decision is taken. Effects, refusals and consequences return through the members' personal Dks as new evidence for local and global synthesis. A single refusal is never a universal individual veto, but it is never mere noise either: every refusal carries its grounds, even when its author stays anonymous. A well-founded pattern of refusals or unexpected consequences can justify review, and so can a single refusal whose grounds bring information beyond the scope considered before the decision, once an advanced triage confirms what that information is and what it means ([veto chain](https://uid.drayker.org)). A decision can then be maintained, adapted, given exceptions, suspended or reversed in light of the new state of the system.

Auditability does not mean total exposure. Personal context, private projects, sensitive research, critical infrastructure and open knowledge can have different permissions; authorized Dk processes may use information that is not generally disclosed, while the existence and legitimacy of the decision path remain reviewable.

Security conditions normal decisions, not only attacks: what may be accessed, what may be executed, how much capacity may be committed, when a decision must escalate to human, local or global review. Security must not become the fiction that immunizes the system against correction: legitimate criticism is not infiltration, internal error is not sabotage, and extraordinary measures leave enough memory for later review.

## Reputation

Contextual reputation is a base layer of the architecture and is present in almost everything: data, relations, functions and members. It is the evolving form of the system's metadata. It can express trust, integration, validation in reality, contribution and use, and each type has its own rules of evaluation, deterministic or intelligent.

For members, reputation is multidimensional memory, not one moral score. It can include learning, care, local cooperation, reliability, contribution, useful dissent, stewardship, failure and recovery. A new member has little history, not proof of little ability. Honest failure, negligence, fraud and sabotage are different relations and must not collapse into one penalty.

A general level may summarize integration; it cannot replace the underlying trajectory. The multidimensional history remains the real source, and the level is a useful compression that the system can always look behind.

Reputation can influence scarce capacity and responsibility. It cannot define human worth, membership or the right to exist.

Dk Global inherits the reactivity of its members and does not erase it; what is learned from it stays recorded. The use reputation of that material comes only from what it teaches as perspective, without being taken as the real. The weighting weighs reasons, not volume: an impulse that arrives without reasons enters as a signal, not as a direction.

### Collusion and capture

Any measure that grants access invites people to optimize the measure instead of the work, and groups can validate one another. The defence follows from how contextual reputation grows: by proving valid against other information and in the real world. Validation therefore counts by its independence. Its weight falls when it comes from people who only validate one another, and rises when it comes from distant, independent contexts and from tests in the physical world. Dk looks for echo in the graph of validations as it does in knowledge.

Proven collusion is misconduct, not failure: it sits with fraud, not with honest error, and the [DAF constitution](https://github.com/draykerdk/daf/blob/master/dafp/daf-000-federation-constitution.md) already sanctions it (DAF-000 §7).

The weight of a validation follows the evidence that continues to sustain it, and the history is never deleted. There is no decay through inactivity, and a justified pause does not remove a level, as with federative points (DAF-000 §3.3 and §3.4).

Whoever vouches for a member, a result or a UID risks part of their own reputation only in proven fraud or collusion, never in honest error, so that vouching does not close the door to new members.

## Scarcity and abundance

Contextual allocation helps build more capacity. As a resource becomes abundant, it should leave the scarcity queue and join a rising common floor. Reputation then moves toward what remains rare, risky or responsibility-heavy.
