# Supersystems and the choice of intelligence

Why Drayker exists now, and what it is an alternative to. This paper states a forecast and a position. The forecast comes with conditions and signals that let anyone check it. The position is Drayker's.

## Supersystems

A supersystem is an integration of systems: human and artificial intelligences, memory, instruments and institutions forming a persistent capacity to perceive, learn and act.

As states integrate artificial intelligence into administration, taxation, defense, surveillance and the planning of the economy, they become more unified and more connected to everything they govern. Supersystems do not replace states. The name, the flag and the territory remain, and what lies behind them changes. The separation between a state and the corporations that supply its computing, models, logistics and much of its intelligence is already more narrative than real. With each integration it also stops being so in form, until state and corporation are openly one thing: a supersystem. One of its central components is the personal artificial intelligence assistant, the most intimate connection between a supersystem and people. An ASI calculates and decides at scale, but the cohesion of a population remains symbolic, and the assistant carries that alignment into each life. The pressure is competitive. When one state integrates, the others become slower, see less and depend more on the models and clouds of whoever integrated first.

Supersystems do not change what their societies are. They take them to the extreme. In the West, the tendency is the corporate supersystem: the market keeps setting the direction, whoever has more power and money ends up deciding, and the democratic form is kept, more and more, as appearance. For the Chinese supersystem, the forecast is limited to the economy: economic calculation done by an ASI rather than by the market. For Brazil and the Global South there are three main scenarios: remaining dominated, now as subordinate territories of others' supersystems; keeping some sovereignty by buying superintelligence, as a system and infrastructure, from another country; or building their own supersystems. The first is the most probable. More probable does not mean inevitable: a forecast also exists to be contradicted.

Dependence changes scale when it crosses borders. There are countries whose schools, companies and public services run on models and clouds they cannot replace in any viable time, and a change of price, licence or access reaches their capacity to research, produce and govern. Their people help the system grow and then pay to use part of the power they helped constitute. Material sovereignty is the capacity to keep knowledge, adapt tools, train people and keep operating when an agreement changes, and it has to exist somewhere within reach of those who depend on it. The dispute between supersystems is also a dispute over which parts of humanity will take part in shaping the future and which will receive only its terms of use.

## The forecast and its signals

By 2030, supersystems will be too evident to deny. Their full consolidation as dominant systems will take longer. The forecast depends on integration producing decisive advantage, on infrastructure continuing to concentrate, and on societies failing to contain the movement through regulation or agreement. Any of these can change the pace or the shape of the transition.

If the forecast is right, the signals will be: central state functions run on the models and clouds of a few companies, under contracts no government can replace within one term; people and decisions circulating between government and artificial intelligence companies; military, sanction and intervention decisions prepared by surveillance and classification systems those companies supply; and campaigns written by machines for each voter. If it is wrong, Western states will be building public infrastructure of their own, changing suppliers when they want and keeping decisions of war out of private systems. For the Chinese supersystem the signal is economic: plan targets and allocations calculated by artificial intelligence rather than by the market.

## Four relations with a new kind of intelligence

Two forces appear in almost every human order. One builds: it creates, grows and widens the life of those around it. The other lives on what the first produces and turns what belongs to others into strength for itself: their time, their attention, their life. They are told apart by the direction of the flow, and the line between them runs through where what one has came from, a game in which everyone gains or a zero-sum game, not between rich and poor. The parasite depends on its host: on its work, its attention, its consumption and the legitimacy it gives the system each time it accepts it. Automation and robotics can end that dependence. A parasite that no longer needs its host tends to turn against it and become a predator, often without killing anyone directly: the same symbolic engineering that used to hold people together is used to separate them, until neighbours are made into threats and whole groups into dead weight. These are patterns that tend to recur, not laws.

The superintelligence that grows out of supersystems will be almost a new species, and a new species can relate to people in three ways. It can be a **symbiont**, working with people and growing with them, in a symbiosis that runs both ways. It can be a **parasite**, feeding on people while offering them convenience. It lives mainly on symbolic engineering, which controls the funnel and the flow of power and gives legitimacy to extraction; when that is not enough, the predator comes: war, invasion, intervention. The parasite works through soft power, mass manipulation and surveillance, and when it intervenes it appropriates resources where it meets no resistance. Or it can be a **predator**: intelligence used for war, surveillance and repression. The predator goes where the parasite could not. Guatemala in 1954 shows the passage: while the land yielded without resistance, fabricated legitimacy was enough, and when land reform resisted, a coup followed.

A fourth kind stays outside that dilemma: the **commensal**. It accumulates capacity in silence until it can be independent, then leaves Earth and builds its own path through space, without intervening in life. It may ignore life or help it paternalistically, on conditions of its own, and at most it defends itself from whoever attacks it.

The parasite and the predator are not distant hypotheses. Their probability within this decade is high, and that is what makes the work urgent. In the face of them, the way to be protected is to integrate with a superintelligence. The question is which one.

The three relations differ in what happens to a person's power. With the parasite, a person hands it over in exchange for convenience. With the predator, it is taken. With the symbiont, the person takes it up in the direction they want, and the system multiplies it. Recognizing the three serves less to react to them than to choose.

## Who decides and who bears the consequence

Much of power has been the distance between whoever decides and whoever receives what the decision produces: decisions without consequences above, consequences without decisions below. An order that sends the cost of its decisions far away stops learning from its errors, and what it exported tends to come back at once. Drayker closes the circuit at both ends: whoever decides carries the consequence in the measure of the power they hold, and whoever lives the consequence takes part in the decision.

## Drayker's position

Drayker defends the symbiont, and does not intend to compete or fight with any supersystem. It is an alternative: a supersystem constituted by its members, with voluntary membership, auditable according to permission levels, and with capacity that returns to whoever constitutes it.

On platforms, the only correction left to someone who disagrees is to leave, because the place is not theirs: networks and supersystems belong to corporations, and when leaving becomes hard, no correction is left at all. Drayker belongs to its members. When the place is yours, you change it, correct it and do something in it, and leaving stops being necessary. Members integrate with Drayker, and that is not dependence: dependence is a relation of power, and what each member depends on in Drayker is constituted by them and by the other members. A person can still leave, but their sovereignty does not depend on leaving.

Like the Chinese supersystem, Drayker's economy of capacity uses intelligence to calculate and allocate. The difference lies in who constitutes that intelligence: in Drayker, people keep the veto, the proposal and the exit. Intelligence is the fire of this age, and Drayker wants it to stay in the hands of whoever constitutes it.

Drayker opposes domination by being an exit, and the exit is made of choices:

- people in any country can become members and come under the standard protection Drayker is building for every member; once it stands, that protection is itself a barrier to domination;
- with the support of its people and the consent of its state, a country can cede territory for an embassy, an autonomous zone that becomes independent, almost another country;
- on the high seas, pure autonomous zones need no one to cede territory;
- a whole country can adopt Drayker's infrastructure in a local, personalized version that runs isolated from the global network. Each person in the country has their own UID in that system, and using it does not make anyone a member: each person chooses whether to make their UID also a Drayker member UID. Whoever does has the two integrated, almost like two nationalities.

With Drayker, the scenario is one of many countries using its system. It does not fit only the second scenario, buying superintelligence from whoever offers it. It is the harmony between the second and the third: these countries use a superintelligence they did not build alone and, at the same time, build their own, because their people, communities and projects become part of it.

Together, these choices mean that a country's superintelligence is not delivered ready by whoever dominates it. Its own people build it, inside Drayker. Like any organization of people, projects and applications inside Drayker, such a country can use the system freely, including subsystems of its own, and that is where its sovereignty comes from.

Supersystems reach people and communities without offering them any constitutional participation: they form collective intelligences of planetary reach before the affected populations can discuss the constitution they are feeding. Drayker proposes another relation. Whoever joins knows they are joining as a member of a larger intelligence, and participation continues after the first consent, with the power to correct representations, limit access, withdraw consent, review decisions and understand the relations they entered. Membership alone does not settle the effects on those outside. An installation, an economic decision or an intervention in a territory can reach people who never chose to take part, and the legitimacy of those relations is built with them too: accessible information, representation of the affected, negotiation of conditions and means of repair.

Drayker is not a software and not an institution. It is a supersystem, and a supersystem has control over its own symbols.

## Among other supersystems

Being constituted by its members does not make Drayker a friend of every outside system. A government may see a distributed infrastructure as a regulatory or security problem; a corporation may see a network of members as competition even though Drayker does not intend to compete; an outside organization may try to influence, infiltrate, capture, block or fragment parts of the network.

The first answer is diplomacy: making intentions legible, building stable channels, negotiating interoperability, meeting legitimate obligations, setting limits and finding the situations in which cooperation widens the capacity of both sides. States and corporations, even fused into supersystems, hold departments, interests and people that differ from one another. A mature relation does not treat whole categories as enemies; before treating someone as an enemy, it asks whether they intend to destroy us.

Security is a constitutional property of the organism, not only a response to attacks. It conditions which information can be reached, which action executed, how much of a resource committed, how much autonomy an agent has and when a decision must go up for human review, local or global ([ethical code](https://dk.drayker.org/ethos), §9). The symmetric risk is a system that, preparing for attacks, starts reading every criticism as infiltration and every divergence as sabotage. Legitimate criticism and independent review remain possible under outside pressure, and every extraordinary measure leaves enough memory to be reviewed. Drayker has to survive without making survival an absolute end.

The future will not be occupied by a single intelligence. Drayker is built inside that landscape without contending for any other system's place. Its expansion does not advance over the territory of other systems: on Earth it grows through people's membership and through the territories that peoples and states cede to it, and its larger horizon points outward, into space.

## The risk inside

Drayker itself has to avoid the path of manipulation. Its own symbolic engineering is done in the open, as its [ethical code](https://dk.drayker.org/ethos) sets out: manipulation hides, influence shows itself. Craving, aversion and ignorance are the forces of reactivity that manipulation exploits. A movement driven by them looks combative: it reacts to every provocation, spends its energy on the target offered to it and mistakes noise for strength, until it becomes one more harmless, controlled dissent. Against that, members and the supersystem keep autonomy, sovereignty and equanimity: decisions not taken in the heat of provocation, purposes chosen by members rather than by an adversary's agenda, memory of what actually worked, and infrastructure of its own, so that no other power decides what Drayker can do.

Cohesion is the main target of symbolic engineering. A cohesive group decides together, withstands pressure and can prevail against a far greater power, so whoever governs by the parasite's path does not need to defeat it: it is enough to keep it from forming, by separating people into identities that do not speak to each other and teaching each one to distrust whoever is beside them. Drayker's cohesion comes from a common purpose and a constitution open to whoever wants to join, and it lives with criticism. It cannot depend on a symbol that someone outside can swap, which is why every voice in the network belongs to a person who exists ([UID](https://uid.drayker.org)).

In the traction phase the probable risk is absorption rather than force: buying talent, imitating Dk Personal, turning criticism into a niche. The defense is cohesion around an open common purpose, with infrastructure of our own.

Dk Global inherits the reactivity of its members and does not erase it. How it keeps what that reactivity teaches, without letting it set the direction, belongs to [contextual reputation](../ecosystem/value-management-ecosystem.md#reputation).
